# Camera shots and chip contacts

Auto view selects one of four fixed corner rigs for each authoritative throw. The selection is stable for that throw, including on late joins. The camera stays at that corner with a small vertical drift and a slow lens change; its target follows the dice midpoint. Both dice stay in frame.

At the recorded settle time, the view cuts above the dice for 1.6 seconds to show their upper faces, with padding for the TV's interface panels. It then eases back to the table. Explicit Table/Room views, manual orbit's existing pause, and reduced-motion preferences take priority. These camera decisions do not change the shared dice timeline.

Strong direct hits can disturb the top few modeled wager chips. A swept, height-aware test rejects glancing, slow, airborne, and late contacts. At most two stacks and three top chips per stack are simulated. Their lower stack and the felt remain collision supports, and displacement, lift, and speed are bounded. Chips recover gently to their betting layout.

This is one-way presentation physics: the recorded dice drive kinematic colliders and the selected chips are dynamic Cannon bodies. The chips do not alter the authoritative dice path, face result, or betting ledger. Banks in raised trays remain outside the dice's reachable bed. The contact simulation is computed once from the recorded throw and sampled by time so rendering frame rate does not change it.

See [betting windows](betting-window.md) for the host-enforced interval that gives players time to bet between throws.
